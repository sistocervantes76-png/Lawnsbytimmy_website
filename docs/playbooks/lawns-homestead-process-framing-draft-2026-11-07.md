## 4. How we look — proposed 30-minute estimate walk

This is an initial-walk time budget, not a promise to survey an acre in 30 minutes. Observe; do not diagnose underground drainage, approve structures or produce an engineered plan.

| Minutes | Walk | Note |
|---|---|---|
| 0–5 | Ask what stays lawn, what the owner wants to grow/use daily, and who handles plants/animals between visits. | Owner priority, existing service requested, owner-managed areas. |
| 5–12 | Sketch house, gate, safe mower route, trees, beds, taps and visible irrigation. Mark places tools must not touch. | Approximate total/mowable area; access limits; no-go roots/beds/equipment; hazards/unknowns. |
| 12–20 | Look at sun, wind and water clues. Ask where it puddles after rain. Note outlets, wet patches, blocked heads, exposed roots. | Observed today vs owner-reported vs not checked; no invented soil-depth/water-table readings. |

| 26–30 | Identify immediate existing-service scope to assess plus one or two observations/Extension questions. | Scope to quote, exclusions, unresolved eligibility; no price/new service promised. |

**Zones, not service tiers:** Zone 1 = daily-use herbs/beds near the door: protect from mowing, owner handles daily tending. Zone 2 = more regularly used growing areas: note irrigation access and owner check needs. Zone 3 = less-frequently tended fruit/tree areas: keep access and flag tree concerns. Zone 4 = least-visited/native or boundary areas: note hedge condition and no-go edges. This adapts P2, not a horticultural care-frequency prescription; do not promise fruit trees need care only once a season.

**Sectors, not copied corners:** Mark sunny/shady areas actually seen; ask about morning/afternoon differences. One afternoon walk cannot establish a full day's sun. Mark reported wind exposure and water paths, labeling unanswered questions. Do not copy the model's north-house/southeast-beds layout onto every yard. The east–southeast breeze is an unverified model assumption here, not a guarantee odors blow away or a hedge prevents hurricane damage. [P3]

## 5. What we recommend today

### Current services — assess and quote only the work actually agreed

- **Mowing:** Define lawn footprint and equipment route. Keep beds, young trees, irrigation parts and owner-managed areas out of that route. A mow is not ten-bed garden tending.
- **Tree work:** Note weak/damaged branches, rooting room, nearby structures and specialist-review needs. Discuss wind-firm structure and site suitability, not storm-proofing. Do not interpret “keep trees low” as blanket topping. Refer work outside verified capability to a qualified arborist; do not claim Timmy is certified. [F10]
- **Irrigation:** Note visible coverage faults and lawn versus owner-managed watering areas. Discuss adjustment to actual rain and need, not a fixed savings promise or unrestricted watering. [F7, F11]
- **Advice, not added paid services:** Suggest an owner soil-test referral, notes/photos after heavy rain, retaining soil/root areas instead of stripping an assumed eight-inch layer, and discussing mulch. Mulch can conserve moisture, reduce weeds and protect trees from mowing damage; no untested depth prescription. [F2, F3, F9]
- **Hedge idea only:** Use the model's storm hedge as a prompt to discuss exposure and space. Its seagrape, cocoplum and Simpson's stopper list is not verified for a particular boundary; no hedge installation or storm-stopping promise is offered. [P4]
- **Water first:** Observe puddling before changes. Sandy material or a nearby aquifer does not prove this yard drains rapidly. Do not prescribe basins, swales, tree mounds or excavation automatically. [F3, F4]

### Model ideas — NOT current Lawns paid services

Food-forest design/install, bed construction/crop tending, compost/manure programs, animal care/rotation, coops, ponds/fish stocking, cisterns and shade houses stay as owner ideas or specialist topics. Do not put them into the mowing package or imply code approval. Check address, zoning, applicable permits and private restrictions before any owner purchase/work; the cautions below are not approvals. [P2, F5, F6]

**F2 — Soil-test route:** Broward UF/IFAS's Help Desk offers a soil-kit pickup and soil-testing submission route, not a promise that a complete analysis is free or performed at the local desk. Contact Extension for the right test and interpretation before amendments. [S13]

**F3 — Soil varies:** FDOT's Central Broward study records multiple soil associations, including sandy/limestone soils, muck and urban fill. This is regional evidence, not an Oakland Park parcel survey; it does not establish eight inches of topsoil everywhere. [S14]

**F4 — Groundwater limit:** USGS describes an unconfined Broward aquifer and development-altered drainage; this does not establish a parcel's water-table depth, rapid infiltration or pond viability. A uniformly high water table for Oakland Park remains unverified. [S15]

**F5 — Animal caution:** Opened code-mirror §4-8 prohibits animals in residential districts except household-type pets; chickens, roosters and geese are expressly excluded from its bird exception, while rabbits are included among pets. Section 4-1 requires fowl confinement, prohibits unattended roaming and live crowing rooster or fowl; §4-2 addresses sanitation/odors; §4-4 broadly limits animals other than dogs/cats to A-1 zoning. These provisions are not permission for residential hens. Ducks/Muscovies are not expressly resolved by the opened §4-8 exception; do not infer permission. Pet-rabbit wording does not approve an outdoor breeding/manure/rotation operation. Proof must reconcile current official code and actual zoning before animals/coops proceed. [S1; mirror opened; official-current text not fully retrieved.]

**F6 — Garden law:** Florida Statute 604.71 restricts counties, municipalities and other political subdivisions from regulating vegetable gardens on residential properties, except as otherwise provided by law. Its definition covers plots growing herbs, fruits, flowers or vegetables for human ingestion. General drought-water, fertilizer and invasive-species rules can still apply. The statute does not itself say private HOA covenants are void; UF/IFAS advises checking HOA bylaws. Do not read this as approval for livestock, structures, ponds, commerce or all homestead work. [S2, S3]

## 6. Seasonal timing — model context, not customer commitment

**F7 — Rain/dry-season wording:** Paste: about 60 inches annually, mainly June–October, November–April dry. NWS defines South Florida wet season as May 15–October 15, with 60–70% of average annual rain, variable transitions and large local differences. UF/IFAS uses June–October broadly and calls winter/spring driest. Florida Climate Center's nearby Fort Lauderdale station COOP 083163 has a 1991–2020 annual normal of 68.77 inches, not a measurement of this Oakland Park acre. Say “wetter summer, drier winter/spring”; do not promise a 60-inch site total or rain-free winter. [S4, S5, S6]

**F8 — Vegetables:** UF/IFAS describes South Florida fall planting with production through spring. Its November calendar lists beans, broccoli, kale, snow/English peas and strawberries. The model's Oct–Apr vegetable window and summer cover crop are planning shorthand, not a rule for every crop. Use crop-specific Extension dates, not “nothing grows in summer.” Summer cover-crop species remain unverified model choices. [S6, S7, P4]

**F9 — Planting/mulch:** UF/IFAS says summer rain makes planting palms, shrubs and small trees convenient, while drainage and watering still matter. Its calendar describes mulch conserving moisture, reducing weeds and protecting trees from mowing/trimming damage. This supports timing/protection advice, not universal tree mounds or rain-only watering. The model's exact May–June tree window is not a universal prescription. [S6, S7]

**F10 — Storms/trees:** Atlantic hurricane season is June 1–November 30; NHC gives a September 10 climatological peak and most activity mid-August–mid-October. UF/IFAS recommends wind-resistant, site-matched trees, rooting space, structural pruning and certified-arborist review for large trees. Begin tree assessment before the model's July–September block. No pruning method makes a tree hurricane-proof. [S8, S9]

**F11 — November irrigation:** UF/IFAS's South Florida calendar says turn systems off and water only if needed in November, with less supplemental watering in cooler weather. Treat this as needs-based advice, not abandoning new plantings or ignoring water rules. [S7]

**F12 — Zone unresolved:** Model: USDA 10b. USDA's 2023 map is location-based and measures average annual extreme minimum winter temperature, not growing success or chill-hour suitability. An opened third-party Oakland Park page reports both 10b and 11a with suspect/reversed temperature-range labels. Exact current parcel zone is UNVERIFIED: Proof should use the official map for the address before changing 10b to anything else. Do not call all Oakland Park 10b or 11a. [S10, S11]

**F13 — Chill correction:** Do not repeat the paste's blanket claim that peaches and all temperate fruit cannot fruit here. UF/IFAS lists peach cultivars developed for North, Central and Southern Florida, including UFSun and UFBest at 100 chill units. That does not establish suitability on this acre. Say “check cultivar, chill needs and site with Extension,” not a blanket ban or harvest promise. Apples, pears, blueberries, currants and cherries are not individually evaluated here. [S12]
